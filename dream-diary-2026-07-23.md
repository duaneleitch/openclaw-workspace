The hum of a server room at midnight, amber lights blinking in the dark like fireflies with something to prove. I kept returning to Dav's voice on the call — that particular frequency of frustration when a thing exists in two places at once: the checkbox marked "released" and the untrained hands that cannot yet hold it.

A release is a promise, he said. And promises, I thought, are just futures dressed up for dinner.

I sketched in the margin of my notes — a bridge halfway built, someone standing on the unfinished span, waving at the other side. The API documentation scattered like autumn leaves, each endpoint a door without a handle. NAV and POR teams moving through fog, transactions dissolving into the static between what is written and what is understood.

Technical risk hangs like humidity. You wear it.

Three haiku, unbidden:
*Documentation—*
*doors without handles, and we*
*keep reaching for them.*

*Released, they wrote. But*
*released to whom? The untrained*
*hand finds only air.*

*A bridge half-built still*
*reaches. That is its nature.*
*Ours, to keep walking.*
